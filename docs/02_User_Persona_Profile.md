# User Persona Profile — Feynman's Mirror

**Project:** Feynman's Mirror (Working Title)
**Context:** HackED 2026 — UAlberta, Solo, 48-hour hackathon
**Date:** 2026-02-16

---

## Primary Persona: The Cramming Student

**Name:** Alex (fictional composite)
**Demographics:** 19–23 years old, university undergraduate, studying concept-heavy subjects (biology, physics, psychology, philosophy, etc.)
**Location:** University campus / studying remotely
**Tech Literacy:** Comfortable with AI chat tools (ChatGPT, NotebookLM, Google). Uses apps daily. Not a developer.

---

### Bio

Alex is a university student who regularly uses AI tools to study — asking ChatGPT to explain concepts, using NotebookLM to summarize lecture notes, watching YouTube explainers. Alex feels confident after these study sessions, but repeatedly discovers during exams that the understanding was shallower than expected. There's a gap between "I read about it and it made sense" and "I can actually explain this from scratch."

---

### Goals

- Verify that they *actually* understand a topic before an exam, not just recognize it
- Identify the specific gaps in their understanding (what parts they can't explain clearly)
- Study more efficiently by focusing effort on weak spots rather than re-reading everything

---

### Pain Points / Frustrations

- **False confidence:** Existing AI study tools make everything feel easy — the AI explains clearly, Alex nods along, but can't reproduce the explanation independently
- **No feedback on explanation ability:** There's no tool that tests whether Alex can *teach* a concept, only tools that test whether Alex can *recall* a fact (flashcards, quizzes)
- **Passive learning loop:** Re-reading notes and re-watching videos feels productive but doesn't reveal gaps — the gaps only surface during exams when it's too late
- **Generic quiz limitations:** Multiple-choice or fill-in-the-blank quizzes test surface recall, not depth of understanding

---

### Behavior & Study Habits

- Studies in bursts, often the week before an exam
- Uses a mix of lecture slides, textbook PDFs, and AI-generated summaries
- Already familiar with the concept of "teaching to learn" but has no structured tool to do it — would previously try explaining to a friend or talking out loud, with no feedback mechanism

---

### Moment of Use (When They Reach for Feynman's Mirror)

After a study session with existing tools — Alex has read the material, watched the videos, maybe used ChatGPT to clarify some points. Now Alex wants to **test** whether they've truly internalized it. They open Feynman's Mirror, upload their lecture notes (as the ground truth), and try to teach the topic to the AI tutee. The grading report shows them exactly where their explanation broke down.

---

### What Success Looks Like for Alex

- A clear score or report that shows which parts of the topic they explained well vs. which parts had gaps
- The feeling of "I can confidently explain this now" before walking into the exam
- Specific, actionable feedback — not just "you got 70%" but "you missed the connection between X and Y"
